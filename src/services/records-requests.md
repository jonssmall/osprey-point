Town records are presumptively open. Many are already published — start with
[meetings and minutes](/government/meetings/), [public
notices](/government/notices/), and [budget documents](/government/budget/)
before filing a request.

## How to file

Submit a written request to the Town Clerk describing the records you want with
enough specificity that staff can find them. Email to
clerk@ospreypoint.example.gov is the fastest route. There is no required form.

A helpful request names a date range, a department, and a subject. "All Public
Works emails about the Dredge Road inlet between June 1 and July 31, 2026" can
be filled in days. "All records about drainage" cannot.

## Timeline

The town responds within **seven business days**, as the state Open Public
Records Act requires. If a request is voluminous or the records are in storage,
you get a written explanation and an estimated completion date within that same
seven-day window rather than silence.

## Fees

| Item | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| First two hours of search and preparation | Free |
| Staff time after two hours | $22 per hour |
| Paper copies, per page after the first ten | $0.25 |
| Certified copies | $5.00 each |
| Electronic delivery | Free |
| Microfilm retrieval (pre-2010 records) | $15 flat |

You get a written estimate before any charge over $25 is incurred, and the work
does not start until you approve it. Fee waivers are available where the request
serves a public interest rather than a commercial one.

## What may be withheld

Some material is exempt or must be redacted, including:

- Personnel records and medical information
- Active investigative records
- Attorney–client privileged communications
- Home addresses and phone numbers of employees
- Security plans and infrastructure vulnerability assessments
- Bid documents before an award is announced

When something is withheld, you receive a written statement citing the specific
exemption. Redacted portions are marked so you can see what was removed.

## If you disagree

You may ask the Town Manager to review a denial in writing within 30 days. You
may also appeal directly to the state Government Records Council, which is free
and does not require a lawyer, or file an action in Superior Court.

## Records the town does not hold

Deeds, mortgages, and recorded plats are held by the **Sedgemere County Clerk**,
not the town. Court records come from the county courthouse.

Two things people expect to be county records are actually handled here in Town
Hall: **property tax assessments**, which are maintained by the town assessor in
the Finance Department, and **birth, death, and marriage certificates** for
events that occurred in Osprey Point, which the town registrar of vital
statistics issues. Certificates for events elsewhere come from the municipality
where they occurred or from the State Registrar.
